Working
 

Using Underpaint

Quick start

  1. Add a photo. Drop one on the box at the top left, or click it to choose a file (JPG, PNG or WebP). Until then you are looking at a sample scene.
  2. Pick a preset and look at the preview. Adjust any slider to fine-tune; the preview updates by itself a moment after you stop moving it.
  3. Download the layered set (.zip), unzip it, and open underpaint.psd in Procreate or Fresco.
  4. Paint on the layer named "Paint here." The outlines and numbers sit on separate layers above it, so your color never covers them.

What each control does

Colors (6 to 64)
How many paint colors the photo is reduced to. Fewer is easier and more graphic; more is more realistic and more work.
Region size (1 to 5)
The smallest shape that gets its own number. Low values merge small shapes into their neighbors for big, simple areas. High values keep fine detail, with smaller numbers.
Smoothing (0 to 3)
Calms noisy edges and speckle. Higher gives cleaner, rounder shapes; 0 keeps every wobble of the photo, which is best for textured subjects.
Detail focus (0 to 3)
Spends more of your colors on busy areas such as stone, leaves and faces instead of large smooth skies. Raise it when a photo loses detail you care about.
Output size
The longer side of the result in pixels. Larger gives smaller, sharper numbers and a bigger file. Smaller photos are enlarged up to 2× so tiny regions have room for a number.
Line weight
Thickness of the outlines: Fine, Medium or Bold. Bold is easier to see on a small screen; Fine suits high-detail results.
Flatten the backdrop
Turns a plain studio background into one flat region, which also removes watermarks and noise on it. Backdrop tolerance sets how different a color may be and still count as backdrop. Turn it off for scenes with no plain background.

The preview tabs

  • Numbered sheet: white regions with outlines and numbers, like a classic paint-by-number page.
  • Color key: every region filled with its final color, so you can see the finished look.
  • Over photo: the lines and numbers on a faded copy of your photo. Use Photo fade to choose how strongly the photo shows through.
  • Legend: every color as a large numbered chip, in the same order as the swatches. Palettes with more than 30 colors are grouped in sets of 30, matching the separate swatch files.

Actual size shows the result at full resolution so you can check that numbers are readable. Click any color chip under the preview to copy its hex code.

Painting in Procreate or Fresco

  • Open the PSD. In Procreate, use Import in the Gallery and choose underpaint.psd. In Fresco, open it from your device’s files. Layers are kept.
  • Fill with the bucket. Select the Paint here layer and fill regions with your fill tool, using the Outlines (set as Reference) layer as the reference so fills stop at the lines and land on "Paint here." In Procreate, open that layer's menu and choose Reference, then drag a color into a region (Color Drop) and use Continue filling for the next. The numbers are on their own layer, so they do not leave holes in your fills. In Fresco, use the Fill tool and look for its option to read other layers. If color leaks past a line, raise the fill threshold slightly or regenerate with a bolder Line weight.
  • Paint by hand. Everything stays on "Paint here," so you can erase and repaint freely without touching the lines.
  • Match colors to numbers. A Procreate palette shows colors without numbers, so keep legend.png in view: in Procreate, open the Canvas settings, choose Reference, and import the image. It floats over your canvas and shows which number is which color. In Fresco, keep the legend open in another window or on a second device.
  • Or pick by number from the canvas. Turn on the Color key + numbers layer, find a region's number, then use the eyedropper on a spot next to the number to pick up its color. Turn the layer off again before painting.
  • Load your palette. Procreate can import the .swatches files (tap a file in the Files app and choose to open it in Procreate). Swatches are in number order: the first swatch is number 1. A palette with more than 30 colors comes as several files; import each one. These files have only been tested to import, not for every Procreate version; if one does not, type the hex codes from palette.txt.
  • Hide the numbers when you finish by turning off the Numbers layer. Keep the outlines if you like the line look, or hide those too.
  • As a base, not a rule. The regions are a starting point. Blend, repaint and add detail on top; the image does not have to look like a paint-by-number.

Tips for good results

  • Start with a sharp, well-lit photo. Blurry photos give wobbly regions.
  • If a face or key object turns blobby, raise Detail focus and Region size, and lower Smoothing.
  • If there are too many tiny numbers to paint, lower Region size or Colors, or raise Smoothing.
  • For a very small output (1000 px), numbers in tiny regions may be hard to read. Use 1600 or 2400 for detailed photos.
  • Crop the photo to your subject first. The tool works on the whole image, so a tight crop spends your colors where they matter.

Questions and problems

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens inside this browser tab on your own device. The photo is never sent to a server. Closing the tab clears it.

Nothing happens, or it says processing failed.

Very large photos on phones can run out of memory. Try a smaller Output size (1000 or 1600) and fewer colors, or crop the photo before adding it.

Why are some regions missing numbers?

Regions too small for a readable number are merged into a neighbor, so every region you see has its own number. Raise Region size or Output size to keep smaller shapes.

Two neighbouring regions look like the same color.

Several close shades can map to nearby palette colors. Lower the Colors setting to group them, or compare the swatch numbers in the Color key.

The backdrop is wrong: either not flat, or flat when it should not be.

Flattening only applies to a plain backdrop that touches the photo's edges and covers a good share of the image. Raise Backdrop tolerance to include more of it, or untick the option for scenes without a plain background.

Can I use it offline?

Once the page has loaded, processing needs no connection. The page loads its fonts from the web, so without a connection it falls back to your system fonts.
